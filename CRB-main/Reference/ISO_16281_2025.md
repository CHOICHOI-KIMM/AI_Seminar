## <span id="page-4-0"></span>**Introduction**

Since publication of the first edition of ISO 281:1990, additional knowledge has been gained regarding the influence on bearing life of contamination, lubrication, internal stresses from mounting, stresses from hardening, fatigue load limit of the material etc. It is therefore now possible to consider factors that have influence on bearing life in a more complete way in the life calculation.

ISO 281 provides a method to put into practice this new knowledge in a consistent way when the modified rating life of a bearing is calculated. However, the calculation method given in ISO 281 cannot consider the influence on life of tilted or misaligned bearings and the influence on life of bearing clearance during operation. ISO/TS 16281:2008 already describes an advanced calculation method, which makes it possible to consider these influences, and in addition provides the most accurate method for estimating the influence of contamination and other factors.

In addition to the content of ISO/TS 16281:2008, this document also addresses the analysis of hybrid bearings with rolling elements made of silicon nitride.

The primary purpose of this document is to provide a unified and manufacturer-independent advanced calculation method that allows for the consideration of actual operating conditions, thus enabling the end user to compare different bearing solutions on the same calculation basis. It is also intended to serve as a manufacturer-independent neutral basis for certification purposes, e.g. as required per IEC 61400-4[[1](#page-41-1)] for bearings in wind turbine gearboxes.

This document is intended to be used for computer programs and together with ISO 281 covers the information needed for life calculations. For accurate life calculations under the operating conditions specified above, this document or advanced computer calculations should be used for determining the dynamic equivalent reference load under different loading conditions.

This document is not intended to supersede other advanced bearing analysis methods that are currently used in the design process as the primary tool for bearing design and selection.

## **Rolling bearings — Methods for calculating the modified reference rating life for universally loaded rolling bearings**

#### <span id="page-6-0"></span>**1 Scope**

This document defines the calculation of the modified reference rating life taking into consideration lubrication, contamination, and fatigue load limit of the bearing material, as well as tilting or misalignment, operating clearance of the bearing and internal load distribution on rolling elements. The calculation method provided in this document covers influencing parameters additional to those described in ISO 281.

The general directions and limitations given in ISO 281 apply to this document. The calculation methods pertain to the fatigue life of the bearings. Other mechanisms of failure, like wear or microspalling (graystaining), lie outside the scope of this document.

This document applies to single- and multi-row radial and thrust ball bearings, subjected to radial and axial load and with radial clearance and tilt taken into account. It also applies to single- and multi-row radial and thrust roller bearings, subjected to radial and axial load and with radial clearance, edge stress and tilt taken into account. References to methods for the analysis of the internal load distribution under general load are given.

The calculation of load distribution and basic reference rating life is also applicable to hybrid bearings, using the dynamic load ratings per ISO 20056-1[[2](#page-41-2)]. The calculation of the modified reference rating life is not applicable to hybrid bearings.

#### **3.1**

#### **basic reference rating life**

rating life associated with 90 % reliability for rolling bearings manufactured with commonly used high quality material, of good manufacturing quality, under consideration of actual load distribution in the bearing

#### **3.2**

#### **modified reference rating life**

rating life for 90 % or other reliability, for rolling bearings manufactured with commonly used high quality material, of good manufacturing quality, under consideration of actual load distribution, lubrication conditions, lubricant contamination and bearing fatigue load limit

Note 1 to entry: Life values for reliabilities higher than 90 % are denoted by the index *n*, where *n* = 100 – percentage of reliability.

#### **3.3**

#### **deflection**

change in position caused by elastic deformation, e.g. in a contact

#### **3.4**

#### **displacement**

change in position caused by rigid body motion, e.g. axial movement of rolling elements caused by tilting of the inner ring

#### **3.5**

#### **initial contact angle**

free contact angle

contact angle at initial contact between rolling element and both raceways, when an infinitesimal small axial load on bearing is applied

Note 1 to entry: The initial contact angle, <sup>α</sup>0 , is generally not identical to the nominal contact angle, <sup>α</sup> , in ISO 281.

#### <span id="page-11-0"></span>**5 Rating life analysis**

#### <span id="page-11-1"></span>**5.1 General**

This clause describes the analysis of the basic and modified reference rating life for ball and roller bearings.

This life analysis is based on the calculation of each rolling element load for the basic dynamic load rating and each force between rolling element and raceway. Methods for the calculation of each force between rolling element and raceway are described in [Annex](#page-25-0) A.

Calculation methods concerning the analysis of bearings of different geometry or for more complex load cases can be derived from the formulae in [Annex](#page-25-0) A.

In the life analysis for multi-row bearings, it is assumed that all rows are symmetrical and have identical rolling element sets. Formulae for multi-row bearings with deviating geometry can be derived from the formulae given in this clause and in ISO/TR 1281-1[[3](#page-41-3)] .

Four-point-contact ball bearings can be approximated as double-row angular contact ball bearings, if these are mounted radially free to take predominantly thrust load, i.e. having only two-point contacts at every ball.

The analysis of internal load distribution and modified reference rating life for bearings of a more complex geometry can be derived from the formulae given in this document. For these bearings, the load distribution for each individual row shall be considered.

There is a discontinuity in the load ratings per ISO 281, which leads to a discontinuity of calculated life results, which also appears in the basic and modified reference rating life. Details are given in <a href="#">Annex F</a>.

#### <span id="page-12-0"></span>5.2 Ball bearings

#### <span id="page-12-1"></span>5.2.1 Rolling element load for the basic dynamic load rating

#### **5.2.1.1** General

The equivalent nominal rolling element load for the basic dynamic load ratings for inner rings and outer rings,  $Q_{\rm ci}$  and  $Q_{\rm ce}$ , are derived from the rolling element load rating  $Q_{\rm c}$  in ISO/TR 1281-1[3]. For all types of ball bearings, the values of the curvature radii  $r_{\rm i}$  and  $r_{\rm e}$  used in Formulae (1) to (4) shall be the same as the values used in the calculation of the basic dynamic load rating.

#### 5.2.1.2 Radial ball bearings

For the inner ring,  $Q_{\rm ci}$  shall be calculated by means of the basic dynamic radial load rating,  $C_{\rm r}$ , for single-row and multi-row bearings.

<span id="page-12-2"></span>
$$Q_{ci} = \frac{C_{r}}{0,407 Z (\cos \alpha) i^{7/10}} \left[ 1 + \left\{ 1,044 \left( \frac{1-\gamma}{1+\gamma} \right)^{1,72} \left[ \frac{r_{i}}{r_{e}} \left( \frac{2 r_{e} - D_{w}}{2 r_{i} - D_{w}} \right) \right]^{0,41} \right\}^{\frac{3}{3}} \right]^{10}$$
(1)

For the outer ring,  $Q_{\rm ce}$  shall be calculated by means of the basic dynamic radial load rating,  $C_{\rm r}$  for single-row and multi-row bearings.

$$Q_{\text{ce}} = \frac{C_{\text{r}}}{0,389 \, Z \, (\cos \alpha) \, i^{7/10}} \left[ 1 + \left\{ 1,044 \left( \frac{1-\gamma}{1+\gamma} \right)^{1,72} \left[ \frac{r_{\text{i}}}{r_{\text{e}}} \left( \frac{2 \, r_{\text{e}} - D_{\text{w}}}{2 \, r_{\text{i}} - D_{\text{w}}} \right) \right]^{0,41} \right\}^{-\frac{10}{3}} \right]^{10}$$
(2)

#### 5.2.1.3 Thrust ball bearings

For the inner ring or shaft washer,  $Q_{ci}$  shall be calculated using the basic dynamic axial load rating,  $C_a$ .

$$Q_{ci} = \frac{C_{a}}{Z \sin \alpha} \left( 1 + \left\{ \left( \frac{1 - \gamma}{1 + \gamma} \right)^{1,72} \left[ \frac{r_{i}}{r_{e}} \left( \frac{2 r_{e} - D_{w}}{2 r_{i} - D_{w}} \right) \right]^{0,41} \right\}^{\frac{10}{3}} \right)^{\frac{3}{10}}$$
(3)

For the outer ring or housing washer,  $Q_{ce}$  shall be calculated using the basic dynamic axial load rating,  $C_a$ .

<span id="page-12-3"></span>
$$Q_{\rm ce} = \frac{C_{\rm a}}{Z \sin \alpha} \left( 1 + \left\{ \left( \frac{1 - \gamma}{1 + \gamma} \right)^{1.72} \left[ \frac{r_{\rm i}}{r_{\rm e}} \left( \frac{2 r_{\rm e} - D_{\rm w}}{2 r_{\rm i} - D_{\rm w}} \right) \right]^{0.41} \right\}^{-\frac{10}{3}} \right)^{\frac{3}{10}}$$
(4)

#### <span id="page-13-0"></span>5.2.2 Dynamic equivalent rolling element load

The calculation of the dynamic equivalent rolling element load is based on rolling element loads  $Q_j$  obtained from the static equilibrium analysis. The basic principle of static equilibrium analysis is described in  $\underline{\mathsf{Annex}\,\mathsf{A}}$ .

The dynamic equivalent rolling element load on a single-row inner ring or a shaft washer,  $Q_{\rm ei}$ , which is rotating relative to the bearing load is given by Formula (5):

<span id="page-13-2"></span>
$$Q_{ei} = \left(\frac{1}{Z} \sum_{j=1}^{Z} Q_j^{3}\right)^{\frac{1}{3}}$$
 (5)

and on a single-row inner ring or a shaft washer which is stationary relative to the bearing load is given by Formula (6):

<span id="page-13-3"></span>
$$Q_{\text{ei}} = \left(\frac{1}{Z} \sum_{j=1}^{Z} Q_j^{\frac{10}{3}}\right)^{\frac{3}{10}} \tag{6}$$

The dynamic equivalent rolling element load on a single-row outer ring or a housing washer,  $Q_{ee}$ , which is stationary relative to the bearing load is given by Formula (7):

<span id="page-13-4"></span>
$$Q_{\text{ee}} = \left(\frac{1}{Z} \sum_{j=1}^{Z} Q_j^{\frac{10}{3}}\right)^{\frac{3}{10}}$$
 (7)

and on a single-row outer ring or a housing washer which is rotating relative to the bearing load is given by Formula (8):

<span id="page-13-5"></span>
$$Q_{\text{ee}} = \left(\frac{1}{Z} \sum_{j=1}^{Z} Q_j^{3}\right)^{\frac{1}{3}}$$
 (8)

For a normal load distribution, i.e. no significant elastic deformation of the outer ring, the difference between the dynamic equivalent rolling element loads for a rotating and a stationary inner ring is less than 2 %. This can generally be neglected, especially as the deviation of dynamic equivalent rolling element loads on inner ring and outer ring partially compensate each other.

When calculations are carried out, the inner ring is generally considered to be rotating and the outer ring to be stationary.

#### <span id="page-13-1"></span>5.2.3 Basic reference rating life

Using the equivalent nominal rolling element load for the basic dynamic load ratings and the dynamic equivalent rolling element loads, the basic reference rating life,  $L_{10r}$ , of a single-row bearing is calculated as shown in Formula (9):

<span id="page-13-6"></span>
$$L_{10r} = \left[ \left( \frac{Q_{ci}}{Q_{ei}} \right)^{-\frac{10}{3}} + \left( \frac{Q_{ce}}{Q_{ee}} \right)^{-\frac{10}{3}} \right]^{-\frac{9}{10}}$$
 (9)

The basic reference life  $L_{10r}$  for a multi-row bearing is given by Formula (10):

<span id="page-14-2"></span>
$$L_{10r} = \left(\sum_{m=1}^{i} \left[ \left( \frac{Q_{\text{ci}}}{Q_{\text{eim}}} \right)^{-\frac{10}{3}} + \left( \frac{Q_{\text{ce}}}{Q_{\text{eem}}} \right)^{-\frac{10}{3}} \right] \right)^{-\frac{9}{10}}$$
(10)

#### <span id="page-14-0"></span>5.2.4 Dynamic equivalent reference load

The dynamic equivalent reference load for radial ball bearings,  $P_{\text{ref }r}$ , is given by Formula (11):

<span id="page-14-3"></span>
$$P_{\text{ref r}} = \frac{C_{\text{r}}}{\frac{1}{3}} \tag{11}$$

and for thrust (axial) ball bearings,  $P_{\text{ref a}}$ , is given by Formula (12):

<span id="page-14-4"></span>
$$P_{\text{ref a}} = \frac{C_{\text{a}}}{L_{10r}^{\frac{1}{3}}} \tag{12}$$

#### <span id="page-14-1"></span>5.2.5 Modified reference rating life

#### 5.2.5.1 **General**

The calculation of the modified reference rating life is only applicable for bearings with balls and raceway made of steel. For details on the calculation of  $a_{\rm ISO}$ , see Annex D. The life modification factor  $a_{\rm ISO}$  per ISO 281 has not been defined for hybrid bearings.

#### 5.2.5.2 Single-row ball bearings

The modified reference rating life,  $L_{nmr}$ , for single-row ball bearings is calculated by means of the life modification factor  $a_{\rm ISO}$  as per Formula (13):

<span id="page-14-5"></span>
$$L_{nmr} = a_1 \, a_{ISO} \, L_{10r} \tag{13}$$

where  $a_{\rm ISO}$  shall be calculated in function of ( $\frac{e_{\rm C} C_{\rm u}}{P_{\rm ref r}}$ ,  $\kappa$ ) per ISO 281:2007, Formulae (31) to (33) for radial

ball bearings, and in function of  $(\frac{e_{\rm C} C_{\rm u}}{P_{\rm ref a}}, \kappa)$  per ISO 281:2007, Formulae (37) to (39) for thrust ball bearings.

It should be noted that in ISO 281:2007,  $a_{\rm ISO}$  is defined in function of  $\left(\frac{e_{\rm C} \, C_{\rm u}}{P}, \kappa\right)$ . For the calculation of  $a_{\rm ISO}$  for the modified reference rating life, the dynamic equivalent load P shall be substituted by the reference load,  $P_{\rm ref\,r}$  or  $P_{\rm ref\,a}$ , respectively.

#### 5.2.5.3 Multi-row ball bearings

The modified reference rating life,  $L_{nmr}$ , for multi-row ball bearings is calculated by Formula (14):

<span id="page-14-6"></span>
$$L_{nmr} = a_1 \left( \sum_{m=1}^{i} \left\{ a_{ISOm}^{-\frac{10}{9}} \left[ \left( \frac{Q_{ci}}{Q_{eim}} \right)^{-\frac{10}{3}} + \left( \frac{Q_{ce}}{Q_{eem}} \right)^{-\frac{10}{3}} \right] \right\} \right)^{-\frac{9}{10}}$$
(14)

where the life modification factor  $a_{\rm ISOm}$  for row m of a multi-row ball bearing shall be calculated in function of  $\left(\frac{e_{\rm C} C_{\rm u}}{i P_{\rm ref rm}}, \kappa\right)$  per ISO 281:2007, Formulae (31) to (33) for multi-row radial ball bearings, and in function

of  $\left(\frac{e_{\rm C} \, C_{\rm u}}{i \, P_{\rm ref \, am}}, \kappa\right)$  per ISO 281:2007, Formulae (37) to (39) for multi-row thrust ball bearings. It should be noted that in ISO 281:2007,  $a_{\rm ISO}$  is defined in function of  $\left(\frac{e_{\rm C} \, C_{\rm u}}{P}, \kappa\right)$ . For the calculation of  $a_{\rm ISOm}$  for the modified reference rating life, the dynamic equivalent load P shall be substituted by the term,  $(i \, P_{\rm ref \, rm})$  or  $(i \, P_{\rm ref \, am})$ , respectively.

The dynamic equivalent reference load of row m of a multi-row radial ball bearing is calculated by Formula (15):

<span id="page-15-3"></span>
$$P_{\text{ref }rm} = \frac{C_{\text{r}}}{i^{\frac{7}{10}} \left[ \left( \frac{Q_{\text{ci}}}{Q_{\text{eim}}} \right)^{-\frac{10}{3}} + \left( \frac{Q_{\text{ce}}}{Q_{\text{eem}}} \right)^{-\frac{10}{3}} \right]^{-\frac{3}{10}}}$$
(15)

and for row m of a multi-row thrust ball bearing calculated by Formula (16):

<span id="page-15-4"></span>
$$P_{\text{ref }am} = \frac{C_{\text{a}}}{i^{\frac{7}{10}} \left[ \left( \frac{Q_{\text{ci}}}{Q_{\text{eim}}} \right)^{-\frac{10}{3}} + \left( \frac{Q_{\text{ce}}}{Q_{\text{eem}}} \right)^{-\frac{10}{3}} \right]^{-\frac{3}{10}}}$$
(16)

#### <span id="page-15-0"></span>5.3 Roller bearings

#### <span id="page-15-1"></span>5.3.1 General

The rating life of roller bearings is calculated on a per-lamina basis to account for the effects of roller profile and uneven load distribution within the bearing. The analysis of bearing internal load distribution using a laminum model is described in  $\underline{\text{Annex A}}$ . The formulae given in this subclause assume the use of a laminum model with constant lamina width. Formulae for the use of a variable lamina width can be derived accordingly.

The number of laminae per roller,  $n_{\rm s}$ , which can be even or odd, shall not be less than 30. If a variable lamina width is used, the maximum lamina width shall not be larger than  $\frac{1}{30}L_{\rm we}$ .

#### <span id="page-15-2"></span>5.3.2 Rolling element load for the basic dynamic load rating

#### **5.3.2.1** General

The rolling element load for the basic dynamic load ratings for inner rings and outer rings,  $Q_{\rm ci}$  and  $Q_{\rm ce}$ , are derived from the rolling element load rating  $Q_{\rm c}$  defined in ISO/TR 1281-1[3].

#### 5.3.2.2 Radial roller bearings

The equivalent nominal rolling element load for the basic dynamic load ratings for inner rings,  $Q_{\rm ci}$ , and outer rings,  $Q_{\rm ce}$ , shall be calculated, respectively by Formulae (17) and (18), using the basic dynamic radial load rating,  $C_{\rm r}$ , for single-row and multi-row bearings:

<span id="page-16-0"></span>
$$Q_{\text{ci}} = \frac{1}{\lambda v} \frac{C_{\text{r}}}{0,378 \, Z(\cos \alpha) i^{\frac{7}{9}}} \left\{ 1 + \left[ 1,038 \left( \frac{1-\gamma}{1+\gamma} \right)^{\frac{143}{108}} \right]^{\frac{9}{2}} \right\}^{\frac{2}{9}}$$
 (17)

<span id="page-16-1"></span>
$$Q_{\rm ce} = \frac{1}{\lambda v} \frac{C_{\rm r}}{0,364 \, Z(\cos\alpha) \, i^{\frac{7}{9}}} \left\{ 1 + \left[ 1,038 \left( \frac{1-\gamma}{1+\gamma} \right)^{\frac{143}{108}} \right]^{-\frac{9}{2}} \right\}^{\frac{2}{9}}$$
 (18)

with

$$\lambda v = 0.83 \tag{19}$$

according to ISO/TR 1281-1[3].

This value of  $\lambda v$  requires a detailed analysis of the contact stress as described in <u>C.1</u>, or by applying the approximate function for the stress concentration given in <u>C.2</u>.

#### 5.3.2.3 Thrust roller bearings

The equivalent nominal rolling element load for the basic dynamic load ratings for inner rings or shaft washers,  $Q_{\rm ci}$ , and outer rings or housing washers,  $Q_{\rm ce}$ , shall be calculated, respectively by Formulae (20) and (21), using the basic dynamic axial load rating,  $C_{\rm a}$ :

<span id="page-16-2"></span>
$$Q_{ci} = \frac{1}{\lambda v} \frac{C_a}{Z \sin \alpha} \left\{ 1 + \left[ \left( \frac{1 - \gamma}{1 + \gamma} \right)^{\frac{143}{108}} \right]^{\frac{9}{2}} \right\}^{\frac{2}{9}}$$
 (20)

<span id="page-16-3"></span>
$$Q_{\rm ce} = \frac{1}{\lambda v} \frac{C_{\rm a}}{Z \sin \alpha} \left\{ 1 + \left[ \left( \frac{1 - \gamma}{1 + \gamma} \right)^{\frac{143}{108}} \right]^{-\frac{9}{2}} \right\}^{\frac{2}{9}}$$
 (21)

with

$$\lambda v = 0.73 \tag{22}$$

according to ISO/TR 1281-1[3].

This value of  $\lambda v$  requires a detailed analysis of the contact stress as described in <u>C.1</u> or by applying the approximate function for the stress concentration given in <u>C.2</u>.

#### <span id="page-17-0"></span>5.3.3 Basic dynamic load rating of a bearing lamina

The basic dynamic load rating of a bearing lamina at the inner ring,  $q_{ci}$ , is calculated by Formula (23):

<span id="page-17-2"></span>
$$q_{\rm ci} = Q_{\rm ci} \left(\frac{1}{n_{\rm s}}\right)^{\frac{7}{9}} \tag{23}$$

The basic dynamic load rating of a bearing lamina at the outer ring,  $q_{ce}$ , is calculated by Formula (24):

<span id="page-17-3"></span>
$$q_{\rm ce} = Q_{\rm ce} \left(\frac{1}{n_{\rm s}}\right)^{\frac{7}{9}} \tag{24}$$

#### <span id="page-17-1"></span>5.3.4 Concentration of edge stress

In cases where the rollers are excessively loaded, or insufficiently profiled for the operating load, or severely misaligned, edge stresses can arise which shall be taken into account in the rating life calculation.

The distribution of actual non-Hertzian contact stress over the length of the rollers can be calculated by means of References [4], [5] or [6] or similar advanced computer programs for non-Hertzian contact analysis, see  $\underline{C.1}$ .

The lamina load for each loaded lamina of each roller shall be corrected by a load correction function, f[j,k], so that the nominal Hertzian contact stress, calculated for a cylinder of diameter and width of each lamina, matches the highest actual contact stress over the width of that lamina, as obtained by the methods given above.

As a first approximation, a load correction function, f[j,k], given in <u>C.2</u>, can be used. An actual analysis of the stress concentration as described in <u>C.1</u> is preferred.

#### <span id="page-18-0"></span>5.3.5 Dynamic equivalent load on a lamina

To consider the actual non-Hertzian contact stress distribution, the dynamic equivalent rolling element lamina load of k at inner ring,  $q_{eik}$ , which is rotating relative to the load is calculated from the lamina loads, corrected for actual contact stress,  $q'_{j,k}$  (see <u>C.1</u> and <u>C.2</u>) given by <u>Formula (25)</u>:

<span id="page-18-2"></span>
$$q_{eik} = \left[\frac{1}{Z} \sum_{j=1}^{Z} (q'_{j,k})^4\right]^{\frac{1}{4}}$$
 (25)

The dynamic equivalent rolling element lamina load of k at inner ring,  $q_{eik}$ , which is stationary relative to the load is calculated by Formula (26):

<span id="page-18-3"></span>
$$q_{eik} = \left[\frac{1}{Z} \sum_{j=1}^{Z} (q'_{j,k})^{\frac{9}{2}}\right]^{\frac{2}{9}}$$
 (26)

The dynamic equivalent rolling element lamina load of k at inner ring,  $q_{eek}$ , which is stationary relative to the load is calculated by Formula (27):

<span id="page-18-4"></span>
$$q_{\text{eek}} = \left[\frac{1}{Z} \sum_{j=1}^{Z} (q'_{j,k})^{\frac{9}{2}}\right]^{\frac{2}{9}}$$
(27)

The dynamic equivalent rolling element lamina load of k at inner ring,  $q_{eek}$ , which is rotating relative to the load is calculated by Formula (28):

<span id="page-18-5"></span>
$$q_{\text{eek}} = \left[\frac{1}{Z} \sum_{j=1}^{Z} (q'_{j,k})^4\right]^{\frac{1}{4}} \tag{28}$$

For a normal load distribution, i.e. no significant elastic deformation of the outer ring, the difference between the dynamic equivalent rolling element loads for a rotating and a stationary inner ring is less than 2 %. This can generally be neglected, especially as the deviation of dynamic equivalent rolling element loads on inner ring and outer ring partially compensate each other.

When calculations are carried out, the inner ring is generally considered to be rotating and the outer ring to be stationary.

For multi-row roller bearings, the dynamic equivalent load is calculated on a per row basis, using the syntax  $q_{eik,m} = q_{eik}$  for lamina k of row m, and for  $q_{eek,m}$  accordingly.

#### <span id="page-18-1"></span>5.3.6 Basic reference rating life

The basic reference rating life,  $L_{10r}$ , for single-row roller bearings is calculated by Formula (29):

<span id="page-18-6"></span>
$$L_{10r} = \left\{ \sum_{k=1}^{n_{\rm S}} \left[ \left( \frac{q_{\rm ci}}{q_{\rm eik}} \right)^{-\frac{9}{2}} + \left( \frac{q_{\rm ce}}{q_{\rm eek}} \right)^{-\frac{9}{2}} \right] \right\}^{-\frac{8}{9}}$$
 (29)

The basic reference rating life,  $L_{10r}$ , for multi-row roller bearings is calculated by Formula (30):

<span id="page-18-7"></span>
$$L_{10r} = \left(\sum_{m=1}^{i} \left\{ \sum_{k=1}^{n_{s}} \left[ \left( \frac{q_{ci}}{q_{eik,m}} \right)^{-\frac{9}{2}} + \left( \frac{q_{ce}}{q_{eek,m}} \right)^{-\frac{9}{2}} \right] \right\} \right)^{-\frac{8}{9}}$$
(30)

#### <span id="page-19-0"></span>5.3.7 Dynamic equivalent reference load of a bearing lamina

The dynamic equivalent load,  $P_{sk,m}$ , of the bearing lamina k of row m of a radial roller bearing is calculated by Formula (31):

<span id="page-19-2"></span>
$$P_{sk,m} = 0.323 Z (\cos \alpha) n_{s} \left\{ \frac{\left[ q_{eik,m}^{\frac{9}{2}} + \left( 1.038 \frac{q_{ci}}{q_{ce}} q_{eek,m} \right)^{\frac{9}{2}} \right] \right\}^{\frac{2}{9}}}{\left[ 1 + \left( 1.038 \frac{q_{ci}}{q_{ce}} \right)^{\frac{9}{2}} \right]} \right\}$$
(31)

For single-row thrust roller bearings, the dynamic equivalent load,  $P_{sk}$ , of the bearing lamina k is calculated by Formula (32):

<span id="page-19-3"></span>
$$P_{sk} = Z(\sin \alpha) n_s \left( \frac{q_{eik}^{\frac{9}{2}} + q_{eek}^{\frac{9}{2}}}{2} \right)^{\frac{2}{9}}$$
(32)

#### <span id="page-19-1"></span>5.3.8 Modified reference rating life

For single-row radial roller bearings, the modified reference rating life,  $L_{nmr}$ , is calculated by Formula (33):

<span id="page-19-4"></span>
$$L_{nmr} = a_1 \left( \sum_{k=1}^{n_s} \left\{ a_{ISOk}^{-\frac{9}{8}} \left[ \left( \frac{q_{ci}}{q_{eik}} \right)^{-\frac{9}{2}} + \left( \frac{q_{ce}}{q_{eek}} \right)^{-\frac{9}{2}} \right] \right\} \right)^{-\frac{8}{9}}$$
 (33)

where the life modification factor  $a_{\text{ISO}k}$  is calculated on a per-lamina basis in function of  $\left(\frac{e_{\text{C}} C_{\text{u}}}{P_{\text{s}k}}, \kappa\right)$  per ISO 281:2007, Formulae (34) to (36).

For multi-row radial roller bearings, the modified reference rating life,  $L_{nmr}$ , is calculated by Formula (34):

<span id="page-19-5"></span>
$$L_{nmr} = a_1 \left[ \sum_{m=1}^{i} \left( \sum_{k=1}^{n_s} \left\{ a_{ISOk,m} - \frac{9}{8} \left[ \left( \frac{q_{ci}}{q_{eik,m}} \right)^{-\frac{9}{2}} + \left( \frac{q_{ce}}{q_{eek,m}} \right)^{-\frac{9}{2}} \right] \right\} \right]^{-\frac{8}{9}}$$
(34)

where the life modification factor  $a_{\text{ISO}k,m}$  for row m is calculated on a per lamina basis in function of  $\left(\frac{e_{\text{C}} C_{\text{U}}}{i P_{\text{S}k,m}}, \kappa\right)$  per ISO 281:2007, Formulae (34) to (36).

For thrust roller bearings, the modified reference rating life,  $L_{nmr}$ , is calculated by Formula (35):

<span id="page-19-6"></span>
$$L_{nmr} = a_1 \left[ \sum_{k=1}^{n_s} \left\{ a_{ISOk}^{-\frac{9}{8}} \left[ \left( \frac{q_{ci}}{q_{eik}} \right)^{-\frac{9}{2}} + \left( \frac{q_{ce}}{q_{eek}} \right)^{-\frac{9}{2}} \right] \right\} \right]^{-\frac{8}{9}}$$
(35)

where the life modification factor  $a_{\rm ISO}$  is calculated on a per lamina basis in function of  $\left(\frac{e_{\rm C} C_{\rm u}}{P_{\rm s} k}, \kappa\right)$  per ISO 281:2007, Formulae (40) to (42).

It should be noted that in ISO 281:2007  $a_{\rm ISO}$  is defined in function of  $\left(\frac{e_{\rm C} \, C_{\rm u}}{P}, \kappa\right)$ . For the calculation of  $a_{\rm ISO}$  for the modified reference rating life, the dynamic equivalent load, P, shall be substituted by the reference load,  $P_{sk,m}$  or  $P_{sk}$ , respectively.

#### <span id="page-20-1"></span>6.1 General

The calculated bearing internal load distribution is significantly affected by the formula set used for the calculation of the elastic deflection in the rolling contacts.

The formulae sets given in 6.2 and 6.3 for the elastic deflection shall be used in the analysis of the reference rating life to ensure comparability of results.

NOTE These formulae sets define the total elastic deflection between the raceways, i.e. the sum of the deflections of both contacts, see <u>Figure 1</u>.

#### <span id="page-21-0"></span>6.2 Elastic deflection of point contact

The elastic deflection of a point contact can be calculated from Hertzian theory. The elastic deflection of a single point contact is given by <u>Formula (36)</u> at the inner ring contact:

<span id="page-21-3"></span>
$$\delta_{i} = \sqrt[3]{4,5 \left(\frac{1 - v_{E}^{2}}{\pi E}\right)^{2}} K(\chi_{i}) \sqrt[3]{\frac{\sum \rho_{i}}{\chi_{i}^{2} E(\chi_{i})}} Q^{\frac{2}{3}}$$
(36)

and by Formula (37) for the outer ring contact:

<span id="page-21-4"></span>
$$\delta_{\rm e} = \sqrt[3]{4.5 \left(\frac{1 - v_E^2}{\pi E}\right)^2} K(\chi_{\rm e}) \sqrt[3]{\frac{\sum \rho_{\rm e}}{\chi_{\rm e}^2 E(\chi_{\rm e})}} Q^{\frac{2}{3}}$$
(37)

The total elastic deflection of both contacts at inner ring and outer ring,  $\delta$ , is given by Formula (38):

<span id="page-21-5"></span>
$$\delta = \sqrt[3]{4.5 \left(\frac{1 - v_E^2}{\pi E}\right)^2} \left[ K(\chi_i) \sqrt[3]{\frac{\sum \rho_i}{\chi_i^2 E(\chi_i)}} + K(\chi_e) \sqrt[3]{\frac{\sum \rho_e}{\chi_e^2 E(\chi_e)}} \right] Q^{\frac{2}{3}}$$
(38)

This leads to Formula (39) for load-deflection:

<span id="page-21-6"></span>
$$Q = c_{\rm P} \, \delta^{3/2} \tag{39}$$

with the spring constant,  $c_P$  given Formula (40):

<span id="page-21-7"></span>
$$c_{P} = 1,48 \frac{E_{\text{st}}}{1 - v_{\text{Est}}^{2}} \left[ K(\chi_{i}) \sqrt[3]{\frac{\sum \rho_{i}}{\chi_{i}^{2} E(\chi_{i})}} + K(\chi_{e}) \sqrt[3]{\frac{\sum \rho_{e}}{\chi_{e}^{2} E(\chi_{e})}} \right]^{-\frac{3}{2}}$$
(40)

For hybrid ball bearings, i.e. bearings with raceways made of steel and balls made of silicon-nitride ( $Si_3N_4$ ), the spring constant is calculated by <u>Formula (41)</u>:

<span id="page-21-8"></span>
$$c_{P} = 1,48 \frac{2}{\frac{1 - v_{\text{Est}}^{2}}{E_{\text{st}}} + \frac{1 - v_{\text{Ece}}^{2}}{E_{\text{ce}}}} \left[ K(\chi_{i})^{3} \sqrt{\frac{\sum \rho_{i}}{\chi_{i}^{2} E(\chi_{i})}} + K(\chi_{e})^{3} \sqrt{\frac{\sum \rho_{e}}{\chi_{e}^{2} E(\chi_{e})}} \right]^{-\frac{3}{2}}$$

$$(41)$$

The calculation of the Hertzian parameters used in the formulae above is described in Annex E.

#### <span id="page-21-1"></span>6.3 Elastic deflection of line contact

#### <span id="page-21-2"></span>6.3.1 General

The formulae for line contact describe the total elastic deflection of both inner ring and outer ring contact of a roller. The elastic deflection of one single inner ring or outer ring contact can be approximated as one half of the deflection given by the formulae below. Thus, the spring constant for a single sided line contact can be approximated as the spring constant for the complete rolling element, multiplied by  $2^{10/9}$ .

#### <span id="page-22-0"></span>6.3.2 Cylindrical rollers

According to Reference [7], the total elastic deflection of a cylindrical roller can be described by Formula (42):

<span id="page-22-2"></span>
$$Q = c_{\rm L} \, \delta_{\rm L}^{10/9} \tag{42}$$

with the spring constant,  $c_{\rm L}$ , for contacting parts made of steel given by Formula (43):

<span id="page-22-3"></span>
$$c_{\rm L} = 35\,948\,L_{\rm we}^{8/9} \tag{43}$$

For hybrid roller bearings, i.e. bearings with raceways made of steel and rollers made of silicon-nitride  $(Si_3N_4)$ , the spring constant is calculated by <u>Formula (44)</u>:

<span id="page-22-4"></span>
$$c_{\rm L} = 42119 L_{\rm we}^{8/9} \tag{44}$$

The load-deflection formula, giving the load on lamina k of roller j,  $q_{i,k}$ , is the following Formula (45):

<span id="page-22-5"></span>
$$q_{j,k} = c_{\rm s} \, \delta_{j,k}^{10/9} \tag{45}$$

with the spring constant,  $c_s$ , for bearings made of steel, given by Formula (46):

<span id="page-22-6"></span>
$$c_{\rm S} = \frac{c_{\rm L}}{n_{\rm S}} \tag{46}$$

#### <span id="page-22-1"></span>6.3.3 Tapered rollers

<span id="page-22-7"></span>

with the spring constant,  $c_{\rm T}$ , for contacting parts made of steel, given by Formula (48):

<span id="page-22-8"></span>
$$c_{\rm T} = 35\,948 \left(L_{\rm we} \cos \beta\right)^{8/9} \tag{48}$$

<span id="page-22-9"></span>

with the spring constant,  $c_s$ , given by Formula (50):

## <span id="page-25-0"></span>**Annex A** (informative)

## Calculation of the bearing internal load distribution

#### A.1 General

This annex describes the analysis of the internal load distribution for single-row radial and thrust ball and roller bearings under radial and axial load, taking into account radial clearance and tilt. Calculation methods concerning the analysis of bearings of different geometry or for more complex load cases can be derived from the formulae given in this document.

The bearing internal load distribution is calculated for a static equilibrium; dynamic effects like centrifugal and gyroscopic forces are considered insignificant. This assumption is generally valid for low and moderate speeds, i.e. typically at  $n\,D_{\rm pw}$  below 1 million mm/min. The consideration of centrifugal and gyroscopic forces is addressed in A.4.

#### A.2 Bearing internal load distribution for ball bearings

#### A.2.1 General

For a radial ball bearing with diametrically measured radial operating clearance,  $G_{rop}$  having an initial contact angle is given by Formula (A.1):

<span id="page-25-1"></span>
$$\alpha_0 = \arccos\left(1 - \frac{G_{\text{r op}}}{2A}\right) \tag{A.1}$$

and having the displacements  $\delta_r$ ,  $\delta_a$  and total misalignment  $\psi$  as depicted in Figure A.1 b), the total elastic deflection of the ball j,  $\delta_i$ , is given by Formula (A.2):

<span id="page-25-2"></span>
$$\delta_{j} = \left[ \sqrt{\left( A \cos \alpha_{0} + \delta_{r} \cos \varphi_{j} \right)^{2} + \left( A \sin \alpha_{0} + \delta_{a} - R_{i} \sin \psi \cos \varphi_{j} \right)^{2}} - A \right]$$
(A.2)

The right-hand side of <u>Formula (A.2)</u> is set to zero if it is negative.

NOTE The initial contact angle,  $\alpha_0$ , is generally not identical to the nominal contact angle,  $\alpha$ , in ISO 281.

In <u>Formula (A.2)</u>, A is the distance between the curvature centres of the raceway groove radii,  $r_i$  and  $r_e$ , see <u>Formula (A.3)</u> and <u>Figure A.1</u> a).

<span id="page-26-0"></span>
$$A = r_{\rm i} + r_{\rm e} - D_{\rm w} \tag{A.3}$$

The distance between the centre of curvature of the inner raceway groove and the axis of rotation,  $R_i$ , is given by Formula (A.4):

<span id="page-26-1"></span>
$$R_{\rm i} = \frac{D_{\rm pw}}{2} + \left(r_{\rm i} - \frac{D_{\rm w}}{2}\right) \cos \alpha_0 \tag{A.4}$$

The contact loads can be calculated from the load-deflection relationship for point contacts using Formula (39). These contact loads act in the direction of the operating contact angle of the ball,  $\alpha_j$ , as per Formula (A.5):

<span id="page-26-2"></span>
$$\alpha_{j} = \arctan\left(\frac{A\sin\alpha_{0} + \delta_{a} - R_{i}\sin\psi\cos\varphi_{j}}{A\cos\alpha_{0} + \delta_{r}\cos\varphi_{j}}\right)$$
(A.5)

#### A.2.2 Static equilibrium

The static equilibrium conditions for the external forces and moment acting on the bearing rings and the reaction forces of the balls yield the equation system, which can be solved by iteration, of the sum of all forces given by Formulae (A.6) and Formulae (A.7):

<span id="page-26-3"></span>
$$F_{\rm r} - c_{\rm P} \sum_{j=1}^{Z} \delta_j^{3/2} \cos \alpha_j \cos \varphi_j = 0 \tag{A.6}$$

<span id="page-26-4"></span>
$$F_{a} - c_{p} \sum_{j=1}^{Z} \delta_{j}^{3/2} \sin \alpha_{j} = 0$$
 (A.7)

and the sum of all moments is calculated by Formula (A.8):

<span id="page-26-5"></span>
$$M_{\rm z} - \frac{D_{\rm pw}}{2} c_{\rm p} \sum_{j=1}^{Z} \delta_j^{3/2} \sin \alpha_j \cos \varphi_j = 0$$
 (A.8)

NOTE The formula set is based on a right-handed coordinate system, where the X-axis coincides with the nominal rotation axis of the bearing. In all figures depicted in this document, the Y-axis is oriented vertically towards the bottom of the page.

![](_page_27_Figure_2.jpeg)

**a) Initial contact angle b) Displacement, misalignment of inner ring and operating contact angle**

### **A.3 Bearing internal load distribution for roller bearings**

#### **A.3.1 Cylindrical roller bearings**

For the case where the raceways are straight (i.e. unprofiled), the elastic deflection of a misaligned roller can be described by a lamina model, as depicted in [Figure](#page-28-0) A.2.

For a relative radial displacement,  $\delta_r$ , of the inner ring, the elastic deflection of the roller j,  $\delta_j$ , is calculated by Formula (A.9):

<span id="page-28-1"></span>
$$\delta_j = \delta_r \cos \varphi_j - \frac{G_{r \text{ op}}}{2} \tag{A.9}$$

The total misalignment angle between the raceways in the plane, shown in Figure A.3, of the roller j,  $\psi_j$ , is given by Formula (A.10):

<span id="page-28-2"></span>
$$\psi_i = \arctan(\tan\psi\cos\varphi_i) \tag{A.10}$$

This leads to the elastic deflection of the lamina k of a straight (i.e. unprofiled) roller j, given by Formula (A.11):

<span id="page-28-3"></span>
$$\delta_{j,k} = \delta_j + x_k \tan \psi_j \tag{A.11}$$

Further, the profile depth is subtracted from the deflection, giving the total deflection  $\delta_{j,k}$ :

<span id="page-28-4"></span>
$$\delta_{j,k} = \delta_j + x_k \tan \psi_j - 2P(x_k) \tag{A.12}$$

The right-hand side of Formula (A.12) is set to zero if it is negative.

NOTE 1 The assumptions in Formulae (A.11) and (A.12) are not exactly true, when axial rib loads act on the roller end face. However, for typical load cases, the consideration of axial rib loads for cylindrical roller bearings is not necessary.

NOTE 2 In case of a profiled raceway, Formulae (A.9) to (A.12) can be modified accordingly.

Reference profiles for typical roller bearing designs are given in <u>Annex B</u>. The use of actual profile data is preferred.

![](_page_28_Figure_14.jpeg)

<span id="page-28-0"></span>Figure A.2 — Lamina model for roller bearing

![](_page_29_Picture_2.jpeg)

Figure A.3 — Misaligned roller bearing

The static equilibrium conditions for the external forces and moment acting on the bearing rings and the reaction forces of the rollers yield the equation system, which can be solved by iteration, of the sum of all forces as given in <u>Formula (A.13)</u>:

<span id="page-29-1"></span><span id="page-29-0"></span>
$$F_{\rm r} - \frac{c_{\rm L}}{n_{\rm s}} \sum_{j=1}^{Z} \left( \cos \varphi_j \sum_{k=1}^{n_{\rm s}} \delta_{j,k}^{\frac{10}{9}} \right) = 0 \tag{A.13}$$

and the sum of all moments as given in Formula (A.14):

<span id="page-29-2"></span>
$$M_{\rm z} - \frac{c_{\rm L}}{n_{\rm s}} \sum_{j=1}^{Z} \left( \cos \varphi_j \sum_{k=1}^{n_{\rm s}} x_k \, \delta_{j,k}^{\frac{10}{9}} \right) = 0 \tag{A.14}$$

#### A.3.2 Tapered roller bearings

For tapered roller bearings, the contact forces are acting perpendicular to each raceway, see <u>Figure A.4</u>. The axial force on the rib can be calculated from the half cone angle of a tapered roller,  $\beta$ . For typical half cone angles, this axial component is rather small compared to the cumulated loads on the raceway and can be neglected in the equilibrium analysis.

![](_page_30_Picture_2.jpeg)

Figure A.4 — Resulting forces on a roller in a tapered roller bearing

For a tapered roller, the total elastic deflection of the lamina k of the roller j, perpendicular to the roller axis,  $\delta_{i,k}$ , is:

<span id="page-30-1"></span><span id="page-30-0"></span>
$$\delta_{j,k} = \left[ \left[ \delta_{a} - \left( \frac{D_{pw} + D_{we} \cos \alpha}{2} - x_{k} \sin \alpha \right) \sin \psi \cos \varphi_{j} \right] \sin \alpha + \left[ \left[ \delta_{r} + \left( \frac{T}{2} - \alpha + \frac{D_{pw}}{\cos \alpha} + D_{we}}{2} \sin \alpha + x_{k} \cos \alpha \right) \sin \psi \right] \cos \varphi_{j} \right] \cos \alpha \right] / \cos \beta - 2P(x_{k})$$
(A.15)

The right-hand side of <u>Formula (A.15)</u> is set to zero if it is negative. Reference profiles for typical roller bearing designs are given in <u>Annex B</u>. The use of actual profile data is preferred.

The static equilibrium conditions for the external forces and moment acting on the bearing rings and the reaction forces of the rollers yield the equation system, which can be solved by iteration, of the sum of all forces given by Formulae (A.16) and Formulae (A.17):

<span id="page-30-2"></span>
$$F_{\rm r} - \frac{c_{\rm s} \cos \alpha}{\cos \beta} \sum_{j=1}^{Z} \left( \cos \varphi_j \sum_{k=1}^{n_{\rm s}} \delta_{j,k}^{\frac{10}{9}} \right) = 0 \tag{A.16}$$

<span id="page-31-0"></span>
$$F_{a} - \frac{c_{s} \sin \alpha}{\cos \beta} \sum_{j=1}^{Z} \left( \sum_{k=1}^{n_{s}} \delta_{j,k}^{\frac{10}{9}} \right) = 0$$
(A.17)

and the sum of all moments given by Formula (A.18):

<span id="page-31-1"></span>
$$M_{\rm z} - \frac{c_{\rm s} \cos \alpha}{\cos \beta} \sum_{j=1}^{Z} \left[ \cos \varphi_j \sum_{k=1}^{n_{\rm s}} \left( \frac{T}{2} - a + \frac{x_k}{\cos \alpha} \right) \delta_{j,k}^{\frac{10}{9}} \right] = 0$$
(A.18)

#### A.3.3 Spherical roller bearings

Spherical roller bearings can be used under misalignment condition between inner ring and outer ring, but cannot support moment loads.

For spherical roller bearings, the load distribution can be calculated from a combination of the calculation approaches for ball bearings and for roller bearings, as depicted in <u>Figure A.5</u>.

Typically, the operating contact angle  $\alpha_j$  of roller j is calculated from the positions of centre of curvature for inner raceway and outer raceway, similar to the operating contact angle calculation for ball bearings. The contact forces between roller and raceways are then calculated using the roller contact lamina model per  $\underline{6.3.4}$ , where contact forces are assumed to be perpendicular to the roller axis. This approach is valid, since generally the contact force components parallel to the roller axis cancel out.

![](_page_31_Figure_9.jpeg)

NOTE Radii are not to scale. Exemplarily depicted is a spherical roller bearing that does not have ribs or guide rings.

<span id="page-31-2"></span>Figure A.5 — Resulting forces on a roller in a spherical roller bearing

The static equilibrium conditions for the external forces acting on the bearing rings and the reaction forces of the rollers on both rows yield the equation system of the sum of all forces, which can be solved by iteration given by Formulae (A.19) and Formulae (A.20):

<span id="page-32-1"></span>
$$F_{r} - c_{s} \sum_{i=1}^{2} \left[ \sum_{j=1}^{Z} \left( \cos \alpha_{i,j} \cos \varphi_{j} \sum_{k=1}^{n_{s}} \delta_{i,j,k}^{\frac{10}{9}} \right) \right] = 0$$
(A.19)

<span id="page-32-2"></span>
$$F_{a} - c_{s} \sum_{i=1}^{2} \left[ \sum_{j=1}^{Z} \left( \sin \alpha_{i,j} \sum_{k=1}^{n_{s}} \delta_{i,j,k}^{\frac{10}{9}} \right) \right] = 0$$
(A.20)

NOTE The operating contact angle  $\alpha_{i,j}$  can be positive or negative.

#### <span id="page-32-0"></span>A.4 Consideration of centrifugal and gyroscopic forces

Generally, for standard bearings operating below the thermal reference speed per ISO 15312[8], centrifugal and gyroscopic forces have little effect on the bearing internal load distribution, and thus can be neglected.

However, at a very high speed, typically at  $n\,D_{\rm pw}$  over 1 million mm/min, the rolling element load distribution can be significantly altered by the effect of centrifugal forces acting on the rolling element.

In this case, the effect of centrifugal and gyroscopic forces on the rolling element should be considered by calculating the static equilibrium for each rolling element. Typically, this is done simultaneously with the calculation of static equilibrium for the bearing ring, which leads to a significant increase of degrees of freedom for the calculation model.

$$P(x_k) = 0,00050 D_{\text{we}} \ln \left[ \frac{1}{1 - \left( \frac{\left[ 2|x_k| - (L_{\text{we}} - 2.5 D_{\text{we}})\right]}{2.5 D_{\text{we}}} \right)^2} \right] \quad \text{for} |x_k| > \frac{L_{\text{we}} - 2.5 D_{\text{we}}}{2}$$
(B.9)

#### **B.6** Tapered roller bearings

Profile of tapered rollers, perpendicular to the axis of the roller is given by Formula (B.10):

<span id="page-34-0"></span>
$$P(x_k) = \frac{0,00045}{\cos \beta} D_{\text{we}} \ln \left[ \frac{1}{1 - \left(\frac{2x_k}{L_{\text{we}}}\right)^2} \right]$$
 (B.10)

NOTE For typical taper angles of  $\beta < 8.5^{\circ}$ , the rounded value of  $0.00045/\cos \beta \approx 0.00045$  can be applied.

#### **B.7** Spherical roller bearings

Cross-sectional spherical raceway curvature radius of outer ring,  $r_e$ , is given by Formula (B.11):

<span id="page-34-1"></span>
$$r_{\rm e} = \frac{D_{\rm pw}}{2\cos\alpha} + \frac{D_{\rm we}}{2} \tag{B.11}$$

Cross-sectional raceway curvature radius of inner ring,  $r_i$ , is given by Formula (B.12):

<span id="page-34-2"></span>
$$r_{\rm i} = r_{\rm e} \tag{B.12}$$

Convex curvature radius of spherical rollers,  $R_{\rm p}$ , is given by Formula (B.13):

<span id="page-34-3"></span>
$$R_{\rm p} = 0.97 \, r_{\rm e}$$
 (B.13)

#### B.8 Thrust cylindrical roller bearings and thrust needle roller bearings

The roller profile is defined in Formulae (B.7), (B.8) and (B.9).

#### **B.9** Thrust tapered roller bearings

Profile of tapered rollers, perpendicular to the axis of the roller as given in Formula (B.14):

<span id="page-35-0"></span>
$$P(x_k) = \frac{0,00045}{\cos \beta} D_{\text{we}} \ln \left[ \frac{1}{1 - \left(\frac{2x_k}{L_{\text{we}}}\right)^2} \right]$$
 (B.14)

NOTE For typical taper angles of  $\beta$  < 8,5°, the rounded value of 0,000 45 /  $\cos \beta \approx$  0,000 45 can be applied.

# <span id="page-36-0"></span>Annex C (informative)

## Consideration of edge stress for roller bearings

#### <span id="page-36-1"></span>C.1 Detailed calculation method for consideration of edge stresses

In cases where the rollers are only lightly profiled or severely misaligned, edge stresses can arise, which is taken into account in the rating life calculation, see 5.3.4.

The typical calculation procedure is:

- calculation of static equilibrium and lamina loads along each roller per Annex A;
- cumulation of lamina loads to rolling element load and moment around centre of roller;
- calculation of contact stress distribution using a non-Hertzian model as e.g. given in References [4], [5] or [6] or similar advanced computer programs for non-Hertzian contact analysis;
- alternatively, the contact stress calculation can also be carried out using cumulated load on the roller and relative tilt between roller and raceway as input values.

These analysis is carried out both for inner ring and outer ring contact of each loaded roller. The result are the vectors of contact stress values  $p_{\mathrm{H}ij,k}$  and  $p_{\mathrm{H}ej,k}$ , where j denotes the roller and k denotes the individual lamina.

From these stress vectors a load correction function f[j,k] for every contact is calculated, where:

<span id="page-36-2"></span>
$$f_{i}[j,k] = \frac{\frac{\pi(1-v_{E}^{2})}{E} p_{Hij,k}^{2} D_{wk} (1-\gamma_{k}) \frac{L_{we}}{n_{s}}}{q_{j,k}}$$
(C.1)

<span id="page-36-3"></span>
$$f_{e}[j,k] = \frac{\frac{\pi(1-v_{E}^{2})}{E} p_{Hej,k}^{2} D_{wk} (1+\gamma_{k}) \frac{L_{we}}{n_{s}}}{q_{j,k}}$$
(C.2)

for lamina of equal width. For lamina with variable width, the term  $\frac{L_{\rm we}}{n_{\rm s}}$  in Formulae (C.1) and (C.2) is replaced by the width of the respective lamina.

For hybrid bearings, the term 
$$\frac{\pi \left(1-{v_E}^2\right)}{E}$$
 in Formulae (C.1) and (C.2) is replaced by  $\frac{\pi}{2} \left(\frac{\left(1-{v_{Est}}^2\right)}{E_{st}} + \frac{\left(1-{v_{Ece}}^2\right)}{E_{ce}}\right)$ .

These load correction functions are used in  $\underline{5.3.5}$  to consider the actual non-Hertzian contact stress distribution by multiplying the load correction factor with the lamina loads obtained from the static equilibrium analysis, i.e. as in Formula (C.3):

<span id="page-36-4"></span>
$$q'_{j,k} = f[j,k]q_{j,k}$$
 (C.3)

#### <span id="page-37-0"></span>C.2 Approximated calculation method for consideration of edge stresses

As a first approximation, a load correction function, f[j,k], determined from contact stress calculations, can be used for the lamina k, as given in Formula (C.4):

<span id="page-37-1"></span>
$$f_{i}[j,k] = f_{e}[j,k] = 1 - \left[ \frac{0.01}{\ln\left(1.985 \left| \frac{x_{k}}{L_{we}} \right| \right)} \right]$$
 (C.4)

where

$$q'_{j,k} = f_i[j,k]q_{j,k}$$
 (C.5)

for all rollers.

This approximation function is valid for the approximated profiles provided in <u>B.5</u> to <u>B.10</u>, and providing the conditions of medium load and a total misalignment of the bearing of less than 4' are fulfilled. For a general calculation, the use of the detailed method given in <u>C.1</u> and of the methods described in References [4], [5] or [6] or similar advanced computer programs for non-Hertzian contact analysis is preferred.

## **Life modification factor,** *a*ISO**, contamination factor,** *e*C**, and fatigue load limit,** *C*u**, and dynamic load ratings**

#### **D.1 General**

The modified reference rating life, *Ln*mr , is calculated based on the life modification factor, *a*ISO , defined in ISO 281. This life modification factor *a*ISO is calculated as a function of the dynamic equivalent reference load, defined in [5.2.4](#page-14-0) and [5.3.7,](#page-19-0) the viscosity ratio <sup>κ</sup> , the contamination factor *e*C and the fatigue load limit *C*<sup>u</sup> .

#### **D.2 Life modification factor**

For multi-row ball bearings, the life modification factor, *a*ISO , is calculated on a per-row basis to better account for uneven load distribution between bearing rows.

Similarly, for roller bearings, the life modification factor, *a*ISO , is calculated on a per-lamina basis, to account for uneven load distribution between bearing rows and along the width of the raceway. The actual calculation procedure is given in [5.2.5](#page-14-1) and [5.3.8](#page-19-1).

It should be noted that the life modification factor, *a*ISO , per ISO 281 has only been defined for rolling bearings made of steel, not for hybrid bearings with rolling elements of ceramic.

## **D.3 Contamination factor,** *e*<sup>C</sup>

When the lubricant is contaminated with solid particles, permanent indentations in the raceway can be generated when these particles are over-rolled. At these indentations, local stress risers are generated, which lead to reduced life of the rolling bearing. This life reduction due to contamination in the lubricant film is taken into account by the contamination factor, *e*<sup>C</sup> , described in ISO 281.

The background to the contamination factor, *e*<sup>C</sup> , is described in ISO 281:2007, 9.3.3.2 and the formulae needed for estimating its size can be found in ISO 281:2007, Annex A. The following lubrication systems are considered:

- circulating oil lubrication with on-line filters;
- oil bath lubrication and circulating oil with off-line filters only;
- grease lubrication.

#### **D.4 Fatigue load limit and basic dynamic load rating**

Formulae for calculating the bearing fatigue load limit, *C*u, and the basic dynamic load rating, *C*r or *C*a, are given in ISO 281.

## <span id="page-39-0"></span>**Annex E** (informative)

## Calculation of Hertzian parameters for point contact

The ratio,  $\chi$ , of the semi-major to semi-minor axis of the contact ellipse is the root of Formula (E.1):

<span id="page-39-1"></span>
$$1 - \frac{2}{\chi^2 - 1} \left[ \frac{K(\chi)}{E(\chi)} - 1 \right] - F(\rho) = 0$$
 (E.1)

with the complete elliptic integral of the first kind,  $K(\chi)$ , as in Formula (E.2):

<span id="page-39-2"></span>
$$K(\chi) = \int_0^{\frac{\pi}{2}} \left[ 1 - \left( 1 - \frac{1}{\chi^2} \right) (\sin \phi)^2 \right]^{-\frac{1}{2}} d\phi$$
 (E.2)

and the complete elliptic integral of the second kind,  $E(\chi)$ , as in Formula (E.3):

<span id="page-39-3"></span>
$$E(\chi) = \int_0^{\frac{\pi}{2}} \left[ 1 - \left( 1 - \frac{1}{\chi^2} \right) (\sin \phi)^2 \right]^{\frac{1}{2}} d\phi$$
 (E.3)

the curvature sum at the inner ring contact,  $\Sigma \rho_{\rm i}$ , as in Formula (E.4):

<span id="page-39-4"></span>
$$\sum \rho_{i} = \frac{2}{D_{w}} \left( 2 + \frac{\gamma}{1 - \gamma} - \frac{D_{w}}{2 r_{i}} \right) \tag{E.4}$$

and curvature sum at the outer ring contact,  $\Sigma \rho_{\rm e}$ , as in Formula (E.5):

<span id="page-39-5"></span>
$$\sum \rho_{\rm e} = \frac{2}{D_{\rm w}} \left( 2 - \frac{\gamma}{1 + \gamma} - \frac{D_{\rm w}}{2 r_{\rm e}} \right) \tag{E.5}$$

and the relative curvature difference at the inner ring contact,  $F_{\rm i}(\rho)$ , as in Formula (E.6):

<span id="page-39-6"></span>
$$F_{i}(\rho) = \frac{\left(\frac{\gamma}{1-\gamma} + \frac{D_{w}}{2r_{i}}\right)}{\left(2 + \frac{\gamma}{1-\gamma} - \frac{D_{w}}{2r_{i}}\right)}$$
(E.6)

and the relative curvature difference at the outer ring contact,  $F_{\rm e}(\rho)$ , as in Formula (E.7):

<span id="page-39-7"></span>
$$F_{e}(\rho) = \frac{\left(\frac{-\gamma}{1+\gamma} + \frac{D_{w}}{2r_{e}}\right)}{\left(2 - \frac{\gamma}{1+\gamma} - \frac{D_{w}}{2r_{e}}\right)}$$
(E.7)

# <span id="page-40-0"></span>**Annex F** (informative)

## Discontinuities in load ratings and rating life results

#### F.1 Discontinuities in load ratings per ISO 281

The factors and assumptions on internal geometry used for calculation of basic dynamic load ratings  $C_{\rm r}$  and  $C_{\rm a}$  according to ISO 281 are slightly different for radial bearings and thrust bearings. Therefore, there is a discontinuity in the calculated rating lives when a bearing with the nominal contact angle  $\alpha=45^{\circ}$  is first regarded as a radial bearing and then as a thrust bearing.

This issue is explained in detail in ISO 281:2007, Annex C, where formulae are given to recalculate radial load ratings from axial load ratings and vice versa, however no method is specified that gives continuous load ratings or life results at  $\alpha = 45^{\circ}$ .

#### F.2 Discontinuities in rating life results

The method for the calculation of nominal and modified reference rating life specified in this document is based on the dynamic load ratings per ISO 281, thus the discontinuity of load ratings also results in a discontinuity of rating life results at  $\alpha = 45^{\circ}$ , i.e. under the same load conditions, life results for thrust bearings will be significantly smaller than life results for radial bearings.

Further the rating life method per ISO 281 uses approximations on internal load distribution and different consideration of reduction factors for radial and thrust bearings, which are replaced by more detailed analysis in this document. Therefore, under the same load conditions, life results for thrust bearings will be significantly smaller than life results for radial bearings

Therefore, care should be taken when comparing the life results of radial and thrust bearing arrangements for the same application.
